Mood board for Conscious Communities

Five visual directions for developmentalspaces.org, each shown through samples rather than described. Look, react, cross out, combine. The site question is not decided by this page; it is decided by what you say after reading it.

The July "warm monastic" decision and the first redesign pass are both on the table again. The problem with the first pass, in your words: an elegant hand-drawn feeling sitting next to blurry, unstaged photographs. This board treats that as the central question: what is the image language, and does everything else follow from it?

Revised the same evening after your notes: nothing we own is fixed (the cover and the five-domains drawing can be redone; Samarra is good but was too much for a cover); no photography will be commissioned; this is about the general idea, not the Bergerac hub; and the Awami Conscious Food book is the reference for the feeling. Direction E below is the result. Directions B and D depend on photography and are kept only for the record.

E

Watercolour

The language of the Awami Conscious Food book, carried to the site. Loose watercolour illustration on a near-white page, a tight-set transitional serif, one flat yellow, a great deal of air. People, buildings, tables and ideas are painted, not photographed. It is warm without being soft, hand-made without being rustic, and it already belongs to the Life Itself family.

Feels like
A beautifully made handbook from a place that takes care. Generous, calm, a little playful, entirely human.
Palette
page
yellow
ink
wash
wash
The page is near-white, not cream. Colour comes from the paintings; the one flat colour is yellow.
Type
The book uses Literaturnaya, a Russian transitional serif, set tight for titles with an italic second line. Not on Google Fonts; the type section shows the nearest web faces (PT Serif is the closest cousin).
Image policy
Watercolour illustration throughout: figures, places, objects, diagrams. No photographs at all, which dissolves the mishmash problem rather than managing it. The Samarra etching can still appear inside as a plate; it simply is not the cover.
What it needs
Illustrations, which is realistic where photography is not: Jennifer Chan has already painted this world for Life Itself. A first set of perhaps ten pieces (a spiral, a house, a table, a circle of people, the five domains, the three streams) would carry the site. The whitepaper cover and the five-domains diagram could be redone in the same hand.
Risk
Watercolour can drift toward "gentle wellness" if the type and layout go soft too. The book avoids this with a tight black serif and hard yellow blocks; the site has to keep that spine.
Serves
The door very well. The Learn stream naturally. The spine (whitepaper, manifesto) if the type stays severe and the paintings stay few on those pages.

What we already own

The material the project has today. None of it is sacred: the cover and the five-domains drawing can be redone, and the photographs need not be used at all. It is listed so the directions can be judged against what exists.

A

Pencil and paper

A drawn world. The site is illustrated, not photographed: pencil spirals, hand-drawn diagrams, engravings, straw paper, one red. People appear in photographs only deep inside, and then always in the same graphite treatment so they belong to the drawn world rather than fighting it.

Feels like
A well-made small-press book about a serious idea. Whole Earth Catalog crossed with a monastery guidebook.
Palette
straw
cream
graphite
vermilion
pencil
Type
A bookish serif with some hand in it (Alegreya, Vollkorn) or a rougher one; a quiet sans for wayfinding. See the type section.
Image policy
Drawings and engravings at the door and through the Build stream. Photographs only in Learn and in articles, always graphite or duotone, always captioned.
What it needs
More drawings. Someone with a pencil (the spiral's own hand, ideally) drawing a dozen more things: a house, a table, a circle of chairs, a door. Without that it stays one spiral repeated.
Risk
Could tip into precious or twee. The cure is restraint: few drawings, large, lots of room.
Serves
The spine best of all four. Says "there is real thinking here" before a word is read. Warm enough for the door if the copy is warm.
B

The quiet house

Photograph-led, but disciplined to the point of austerity. One good photograph per page, graded into a single muted register (stone, olive, linen), a small centred serif, and nothing else. The place is the argument.

Feels like
Arriving at a place at dusk and being shown to a room. Slow, sure, unhurried.
Palette
linen
olive
stone
black
brick
Type
A restrained text serif with a fine italic: Newsreader, Spectral, or the existing EB Garamond at small sizes. Centred at the door, left-aligned inside.
Image policy
Photographs only, every one graded the same way, and far fewer of them. No drawings except the spiral as a small mark.
What it needs
A photographer. Two days at Bergerac in good light would produce twenty images the whole site could run on. Until then this direction is a promise, not a site.
Risk
Without discipline it becomes the retreat-centre template (Esalen, Schumacher, Embercombe). With discipline it depends entirely on photographs we do not yet have.
Serves
The door and the Learn stream well. The spine less well: a whitepaper wants ink, not a landscape.
C

The journal

Take the "publishing home" posture literally: the site is a small press. Strong serif display, white paper, a black-and-red printer's palette, engravings and commissioned illustration as art, articles and documents as the unit. Photographs appear only inside reportage, credited like a magazine would.

Feels like
A journal you would subscribe to. Issues, essays, documents. A place that publishes rather than promotes.
Palette
white
ink
red
paper
Type
A display serif with real bite (Fraunces at its sharp end, Newsreader at 72 optical size, or a Bodoni) over a calm text serif. Small caps for datelines.
Image policy
Engravings and commissioned illustration as art. Photographs only within articles and always credited. No hero photography.
What it needs
A steady flow of things to publish, which the backlog has. An engraving library, which is free. Occasional illustration commissions.
Risk
Cold at the door. A person wondering whether to live in community may not feel welcomed by a journal. Needs the warmest copy of the four.
Serves
The spine and the Build and Fund streams superbly. Matches the ratified posture ("a working library, not a brochure") most honestly.
D

Warm and civic

Colour, people, plain language. Photo tiles of real life, a friendly sans beside a serif, the Life Itself yellow somewhere in the family, rounded and open. The direction the old consciouscoliving.org was reaching for, done well.

Feels like
Being invited to dinner. Open door, first names, nobody in robes.
Palette
cream
yellow
terracotta
leaf
charcoal
Type
A humanist sans doing most of the work (Work Sans, Atkinson Hyperlegible) with a friendly serif for headlines.
Image policy
Lots of photographs in colour, so they must be good. The current ones would need replacing almost entirely.
What it needs
Photography, again, and more of it than B. And discipline about colour, or it becomes "wellness".
Risk
This is the direction the July decision rejected as "indistinguishable from generic conscious-living content". That judgement still seems right for the front door.
Serves
The curious visitor best. The spine worst.

The photograph question

The mishmash has three honest resolutions. Each is shown on the same image so the difference is the treatment, not the subject.

Photography is not going to be commissioned, and this is not about one hub, so the honest answer is the fourth one: no photographs on the front door at all. Directions A and E are built without them. If photographs ever appear, they are of many places, treated one way, deep inside.

Copy, noted and parked

The words on the first pass are not right, and that is a separate job from the design. What is wrong, so it is not forgotten:

  • "Developmental spaces where people grow" above "Places where people grow, together" repeats "people grow" and asks the reader to accept "developmental space" before it has been explained.
  • The lede lists formats (co-living houses, retreat centres, monastery, university) before saying what they have in common. The common thing should come first.
  • "Exploring them, building them and funding them" is the site's structure showing through. A visitor needs to feel addressed before they are routed.
  • The stream names ("Learn: living together, on purpose") are headings that explain the site rather than sentences that speak to someone.

Suggested handling: once the direction is chosen, one working session on the hero and the three door sentences, with you writing and me editing rather than the other way round.

Links to look at directly

Screenshots flatten things. These are worth opening.

  • Dark Mountain Project: engravings, one accent, a press with a world. (A, C)
  • Plum Village: hand-drawn logotype, split layout, real people. (A, B)
  • Monastic Academy: one photograph, an olive wash, a line of text. (B)
  • San Francisco Zen Center: red enso, sepia portrait, small caps. (B)
  • Deep Springs College: place and people in one frame. (B)
  • Noema and Aeon: editorial authority, illustration over photography. (C)
  • The Point: the physical object as hero. (C)
  • Taizé and Findhorn: warm, humane, photographic. (D)
  • Life Itself and Archipelago: the family, to rhyme with and not copy.
  • Print, for the shelf: the Whole Earth Catalog (working library, hand-made, serious); Dark Mountain's books; Emergence Magazine in print; any Taschen facsimile of a medieval herbal, for how drawings and text share a page.

What would help most from you

  • Is E the direction, or E carrying some of A (pencil for the mark and diagrams, watercolour for figures and places) or of C (the journal's severity on the whitepaper and manifesto pages)?
  • Is a small illustration commission with Jennifer Chan realistic, and roughly when? The mockups can use the existing Awami paintings as stand-ins meanwhile.
  • Type: does the tight black serif of the book (PT Serif or Prata on the web) feel right for the site, or should the site be a touch softer than the book?
  • Yellow: the book's flat yellow is Life Itself's. Keep it as the family tie, or give Conscious Communities its own single colour?
